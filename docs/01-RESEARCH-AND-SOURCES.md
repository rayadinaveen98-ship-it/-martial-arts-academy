## Primary coaching reference
**England Boxing, Boxing Coaching Handbook, Part 1 (February 2026).**
https://www.englandboxing.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/EB_Boxing-Coaching-Handbook-Part-1_09_02_26.pdf

Key implications for product structure (editorial summary):
- Safety, coaching suitability, and progression are foundational.
- Stance, guard, footwork, balance and coordinated movement underlie more demanding techniques.
- Coach demonstration and feedback are central; merely watching a clip is not enough.
- In-app exercises should be carefully graded; no contact coaching without appropriate supervision.

## Physical activity and beginner load
**World Health Organization — Physical activity**
https://www.who.int/health-topics/noncommunicable-diseases/physical-activity

Implications: Inactive users should start with small amounts of activity and gradually increase frequency, duration and intensity. Do not equate an arbitrary streak with fitness progression.

## Pose estimation: potential, not technique validation
**Google ML Kit pose detection (Android)**
https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/pose-detection/android

The API estimates 33 pose landmarks. This does not independently establish whether a boxing movement is technically correct. Camera feedback is a separate future research workstream, **excluded from v0.1**.

## Research taxonomy for later disciplines
- Boxing: stance, guard, footwork, punch mechanics and defensive awareness.
- Kickboxing: stance and balance plus kicks and punches, subject to the specific ruleset.
- Karate / Taekwondo / Muay Thai: distinct pedagogical and technical standards.
- Grappling systems: breakfalls, throws, locks and submissions require additional supervision and safety controls.

**Do not import movements from one discipline into another without explicit context.**

## Movement-specification checklist (for each instructional asset)
1. Discipline / ruleset / stance orientation.
2. Skill prerequisites and intended learning objective.
3. Camera viewpoints needed to reveal relevant mechanics.
4. Start position, sequential actions, stable end/recovery position.
5. Guard and balance cues, likely novice errors.
6. Drill boundaries, fatigue and stop conditions.
7. Reference source and rights status.
8. Named instructor/coach reviewer, review date, pass/fail disposition.
