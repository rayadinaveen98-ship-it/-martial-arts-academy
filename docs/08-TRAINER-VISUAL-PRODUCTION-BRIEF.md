# 08 — Trainer character and technique visualization production brief

**Status:** Proposed asset-production process; no character selected and no animations approved.

## Why we cannot begin with AI fighter images
Beautiful synthetic fighters often display incorrect hand anatomy, joint placement, foot orientation or transitions between poses. A single polished still cannot establish a physically valid technique across frames. We must preserve the principle: **instructional truth is more important than spectacle**.

## Recommended two-layer visual strategy
1. **Cinematic shell:** premium character identity, arena lighting, visual atmosphere and emotional presentation. Decorative character depictions can be concept-only and must not be mistaken for actionable instruction.
2. **Vetted technique layer:** rights-cleared real trainer footage as the safest early option, or a reviewed 3D animation built from licensed trainer references. Only this layer can visually teach physical execution.

No promise of 'flawless' is made without qualified external review.

## Candidate technique-capture shoot list
**Capture contract for each of BOX-001 / BOX-002 / BOX-003:**
- Qualified coach plus performing trainer; documented credentials and permissions.
- Neutral clothing contrasted with background so hands, ankles, elbows, shoulders and feet remain visible.
- Camera on stable tripod with full-body framing including ground contact.
- Straight-on, side and three-quarter takes; optional close-ups for hands/feet **only after technical approval**.
- Both orthodox and southpaw, recorded and individually reviewed. Do not assume mechanically mirrored footage is sufficient.
- Start -> full motion -> recovery without cuts, then slow instructed repetition and still keyframes.
- Capture natural timing and optional spoken cues; record any context the coach says is essential.
- Get explicit consent and perpetual/appropriate usage rights before licensing to the app.
- Avoid scene lighting that hides joint articulation or guard position; decorative lighting is secondary.

## Frame acceptance checklist
- Frame 0 and final frame show comparable balanced guard/base.
- No hand swapping or erroneous orientation switch.
- Smooth plausible transition between key poses, no impossible shoulder/elbow/wrist/hip rotation.
- Feet do not cross, teleport or slide implausibly during static phase.
- Camera angle visually supports the instruction in the adjacent text.
- Playback at 0.5x/normal speeds preserves intelligibility; no semantic gaps.
- No copied copyrighted images/diagrams from federation manuals.
- Corrective examples clearly labelled as **common errors**, and only after expert review.
- Reviewer comments and exact approved revision stored as metadata.
